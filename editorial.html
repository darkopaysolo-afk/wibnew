Sources and editorial policy

How WhoInventedBitcoin.com distinguishes documents, estimates, interpretations, and unsupported identity claims.

Reviewed October 29, 2026 · By WhoInventedBitcoin.com · Editorial policy

Our purpose

We are an independent educational resource about Bitcoin’s creation. We aim to help readers inspect the evidence themselves. We do not claim to have identified Satoshi.

How to read our labels

  • Documented: supported by a linked paper, original account, archived correspondence, or official decision.
  • Estimate: derived from a method with assumptions and uncertainty.
  • Interpretation: our explanation of what the available material may mean.
  • Unconfirmed theory: a proposed explanation that does not establish identity.

Sources come first

We prefer original papers, correspondence published by participants, direct statements, and court documents. An archive hosts a document; it does not independently authenticate everything in it. We link the source so readers can evaluate its context.

Dates and corrections

A review date records an editorial review, not a guarantee that every external data source is current. Market data carries its own timestamp. Historical records are not presented as breaking news.

Found an error? Contact us with the page URL, the disputed statement, and a source. We can then assess and correct it.

Authorship and independence

Articles are published under the WhoInventedBitcoin.com name. We do not invent expert reviewers, personal research credentials, or numerical probabilities for candidates. Advertising is separate from our evidence assessments.

Privacy and respectful research

We examine public claims and documents. A person's home, relatives, or private contact details are not needed to understand Bitcoin's history.
