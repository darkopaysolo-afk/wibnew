Wei Dai and the Satoshi question

Dai wrote b-money, another proposal cited by Bitcoin. His work belongs in the history without assuming he was Satoshi.

Reviewed October 29, 2026 · By WhoInventedBitcoin.com · Editorial policy

WD
Evidence status

Cited researcher; unconfirmed identity theory

What is b-money?

Wei Dai's 1998 proposal describes pseudonymous participants keeping account records and transferring value through signed messages. It explores how a community could exchange money without conventional institutions.

Why is Dai part of this story?

Bitcoin's whitepaper cites b-money. Readers can inspect the proposal directly and see the earlier questions about distributed accounts, computational work, and cooperation.

Where does the identity theory fall short?

Authorship of a cited proposal is evidence of a contribution to the field, not evidence of using a particular pseudonym. An identity claim still needs a connection to the creation of Bitcoin itself.

Our assessment

Documented predecessor; identity unconfirmed. Dai's place in the history does not depend on solving the mystery. Separate what the citation proves from what later speculation adds.
