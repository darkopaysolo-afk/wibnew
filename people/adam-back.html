What is the documented connection?

Back's Hashcash paper describes a computational cost used to discourage abuse of internet resources. Bitcoin's whitepaper cites that work. That provides a direct, checkable link between the research and Bitcoin's design.

The public record

His emails with Satoshi

On August 20, 2008, Satoshi emailed Adam Back to check the Hashcash citation and shared a link to a draft of the Bitcoin paper. Back replied the next day and pointed Satoshi toward Wei Dai’s b-money proposal. The correspondence is described and quoted in the High Court’s judgment in COPA v Wright, including paragraphs 23.2 and 661–666.

Read the court’s account of the emails →

What about forum discussions?

Back posted on Bitcointalk under the username adam3us. In his April 18, 2013 introduction, he identified himself as the inventor of Hashcash and recalled suggesting the b-money reference when Satoshi emailed him in 2008.

This is a later forum account of earlier email correspondence. It should not be presented as a public forum conversation between Back and Satoshi. The records document contact between the names; they do not by themselves settle the identity behind the Satoshi pseudonym.

Read Adam Back’s original forum introduction →

Why people propose Back

The theory rests partly on his familiarity with proof of work and the technical environment around digital cash. Those are relevant qualifications, but they are shared by more than one researcher.

What is missing?

A precursor paper does not establish authorship of a later system. A persuasive identity claim needs independently verifiable evidence connecting a person to Satoshi's work, rather than simply to an idea the work used.

Inside the evidence file

Read the correspondence in sequence

August 2008 · email / April 2013 · forum recollection

The judgment discusses Satoshi’s August 20 email about citing Hashcash and Back’s August 21 reply pointing to b-money. Back’s later adam3us forum post recalls that contact. These are distinct records created at different times.

Read paragraphs 661–666 of the judgment →

What the record leaves open

The correspondence documents a technical connection. Our assessment: it does not establish Back’s authorship of Bitcoin, and the later forum recollection does not demonstrate a public forum conversation with Satoshi. Keep the date, source type, and identity claim separate.

Source summary above; the assessment is our editorial interpretation. Reviewed October 29, 2026.

Our assessment

Documented influence; identity unconfirmed. Begin with Hashcash itself. Understanding the mechanism is more useful than treating a bibliography entry as a secret signature.
