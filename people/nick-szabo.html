What did Nick Szabo contribute?

Szabo's published Bit Gold proposal describes using costly computation and records of ownership to construct digital scarcity. The version linked below was published in 2005; accounts of the idea's earlier origins should not be confused with that publication date.

Why the theory attracts attention

Bitcoin and Bit Gold address related questions: how can digital objects be scarce, and how can ownership be recorded without relying entirely on an intermediary? The overlap makes Szabo relevant to Bitcoin's intellectual history.

What the theory does not establish

A design resemblance does not identify an author. It can also reflect influence, shared research, or independent work on the same problem. Writing-style comparisons depend on the samples and methods selected; they should not be treated as an identity test.

Our assessment

Relevant predecessor; identity unconfirmed. Compare the actual proposals before drawing conclusions from biographical similarities. Influence and authorship require different evidence.
