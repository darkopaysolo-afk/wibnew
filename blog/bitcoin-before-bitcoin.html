Bitcoin before Bitcoin: Hashcash, b-money, and Bit Gold

Three earlier proposals help explain the questions Bitcoin addressed—and why influence is different from authorship.

Reviewed October 29, 2026 · By WhoInventedBitcoin.com · Editorial policy

Read the predecessors on their own terms

Calling an idea a Bitcoin precursor can hide what its author was actually trying to solve. The useful comparison is between mechanisms, rather than a contest to award the entire invention to one earlier proposal.

Hashcash: make abuse costly

Adam Back's Hashcash work describes requiring computational effort to create a token that is easier to check than to produce. Its original applications included discouraging spam and other abuse of internet resources.

b-money: accounts without ordinary identities

Wei Dai's b-money describes protocols in which pseudonymous participants maintain accounts and authorize transfers. Its discussion explores money creation, signed messages, and cooperation without conventional institutions.

Bit Gold: computation and digital scarcity

Nick Szabo's Bit Gold explores turning costly computation into objects with recorded ownership. The linked text was published in 2005. It is useful to compare its proposed records and transfer process with later approaches.

What changes in Bitcoin?

Bitcoin's whitepaper cites both Hashcash and b-money. It sets out a network using proof of work to establish an agreed transaction history and make conflicting spending difficult.

What the comparison tells us

Our interpretation is that the predecessor texts make Bitcoin easier to understand: computational cost, public records, and signatures each address different parts of the problem. The important question is how those parts work together.

They also offer a lesson for the identity investigation. A citation proves that a work was acknowledged; it does not prove that its author was secretly the author of the citing paper. Shared ideas should lead readers toward the documents, rather than directly to a conclusion about a pseudonym.

Check the sources

  2. Wei Dai — b-money (1998)
  3. Nick Szabo — Bit gold (2005 publication)
  4. Satoshi Nakamoto — Bitcoin whitepaper (2008)
